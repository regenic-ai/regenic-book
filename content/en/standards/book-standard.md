# Public book standards

> Public edition for contributors and readers. Full editorial decision logs and change ledgers are not published in this repository.

## 1. Product standards: the five-part business book

Draw the median pattern from classics such as *Crossing the Chasm*,
*The Innovator's Dilemma*, *Good to Great*, *The Lean Startup*, *Zero to One*,
and *Blue Ocean Strategy*:

1. **One counter-intuitive core claim** — one sentence, falsifiable.
2. **One reusable master framework** — something readers can sketch on a whiteboard.
   Regenic uses the dual-capability model (judgment standards + shared context).
3. **Three to five named concepts** — at most five tier-one concepts; **Rewrite the
   DNA / Regenic** is the primary outward concept. See `outline.md` for the full map.
4. **Cases that stress-test the claim** — winners and losers; at least one external
   comparison per chapter or an explicit missing side.
5. **An executable playbook** — something a leader can run on Monday morning. Regenic
   uses the 90-day manual in Chapter 13.

**Primary reader**: the accountable leader (founder, CEO, or business-unit head).

**Carrier relationship**: the book is the current open complete argument; Regenic.ai
is the living carrier; bioby.ai is the first internal experiment field, not the
methodology homepage.

**Service boundary**: Regenic helps identify the limiting resource, encode standards,
freeze acceptance criteria, and run the first validation cycle. It does not replace
the leader's judgment, ghostwrite detached standards, or outsource the transformation.

## 2. Public naming standard

- Primary title: **Rewrite the DNA / Regenic**
- Subtitle: **How to Build an AI-Native Organization**
- *The Last Scarcity* remains a tier-one concept and potential sequel title, not the
  primary book title.

Title scoring and market tests are maintained outside the public repository.

## 3. Chapter acceptance criteria

Every finished chapter must satisfy all of the following:

1. The claim can be restated in one sentence and follows from the book's core claim.
2. The chapter includes a whiteboard-ready framework.
3. At least one external winner/loser comparison with citable data; report medians
   separately for each side.
4. At least three quotable candidate lines.
5. A "what to do Monday morning" action list at the end.
6. Body paragraphs pass [`prose-standard.md`](./prose-standard.md) fluency
   acceptance (four prohibitions, aside/forced-metaphor rules, author–year cites).

## 4. Writing workflow (10/10/80)

- **10% standards**: this file + `outline.md` + [`prose-standard.md`](./prose-standard.md).
- **10% evidence**: public research index + private case library (internal cases
  cannot be the only evidence).
- **80% optimization**: one-page brief → logic pass → chapter expansion →
  clear asides and hard joins against the prose standard.

Chapter state machine: `one-pager → drafting → complete → open public revision`.

## 5. Public success criteria

Long term: do the core concepts become organizational language; how many teams finish
a first cycle with the standards; does Regenic.ai keep publishing testable success,
failure, and boundary cases.
